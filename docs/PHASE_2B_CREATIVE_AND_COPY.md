---

**1. EUROPE IS NOT ONE FOOTBALL MARKET.**
"Europe" is dozens of football markets, each with its own level, calendar, registration rules,
style and demand for players. A player who fits one may be completely wrong for another. The
useful question isn't "can my child play in Europe?" It's *which market, at which level, at which
moment?*

**2. A HIGHLIGHT REEL CAN'T ANSWER A CAREER QUESTION.**
Highlights show the best moments. A career decision needs the rest: positioning, decisions without
the ball, consistency over 90 minutes. That's why the assessment requires a full match.

**3. AGE, TIMING AND THE TRANSFER WINDOW.**
Clubs register players in two windows a year. Arriving at the wrong moment can waste months. And
for under-18s, international moves are tightly restricted by FIFA's rules — an honest adviser will
explain that before anything else. [INTERNAL NOTE: minors wording to be legal-checked before publication — FIFA RSTP Art. 19 generally restricts international transfers of under-18s, with narrow exceptions.]

**4. PASSPORTS CHANGE THE MAP.**
An EU passport can open doors that are closed to non-EU players, and the rules differ by country
and division. We assess what your child's passport(s) actually mean — and where ancestry-based
eligibility might be worth checking with a citizenship lawyer.

**5. TRIALS, ACADEMIES AND AGENTS: CHECK BEFORE YOU PAY.**
Some opportunities are excellent. Some aren't what they appear. Programme members get opportunity,
trial, academy and agent vetting; the assessment itself will tell you what to look for.

**6. A CONTRACT IS A COMMITMENT.**
An offer is exciting. It's also a legal document with obligations, termination terms and
consequences. Marks is a lawyer [FACT PENDING — E6] and has supported players whose contracts went
wrong. Formal legal review is a separate service [FACT PENDING — E9].

### Worked examples [PUBLIC COPY — all fictional]

**Worked example 1 — "The passport isn't the plan."**
*Fictional player.* 19 · US college, second year · holds an EU passport · few competitive minutes this season · highlights only, no full match.
**Question:** Should he leave college and go to Europe now?
**What we'd examine:** evidence of current level (limited without minutes or a full match) · position depth · what the passport genuinely changes · eligibility and education status · timing relative to windows.
**Reasoning:** An EU passport is a real asset, but it doesn't replace evidence. Without regular minutes and a full match, no honest adviser can place him in a level range with confidence, and clubs can't either. Moving now would mean arriving without the evidence clubs need.
**Decision:** **PLAY MORE → WAIT.** Priority one is minutes and a filmed full match; revisit before a later window with real evidence. The passport stays an asset, not a shortcut.

**Worked example 2 — "The trial that needed a second look."**
*Fictional player.* 22 · finished college · no second passport · offered a European "trial" that requires an upfront fee.
**Question:** Should he pay and go?
**What we'd examine:** who is organising the trial, and what's actually included · whether a club is genuinely involved and in what capacity · what non-EU registration would mean at that level · total cost including travel and accommodation · whether his level fits the market.
**Reasoning:** A fee doesn't automatically mean a scam — but it means the questions must be answered before any money moves. If they can't be, the opportunity isn't ready to be trusted.
**Decision:** **READ IT AGAIN → SAY NO** until the questions are answered; redirect to markets that fit a non-EU profile.

**Worked example 3 — "Europe or college at 17."**
*Fictional player.* 17 · strong youth level · family weighing a European academy against a college scholarship.
**Question:** Which way?
**What we'd examine:** international-transfer rules for under-18s · what the academy actually offers and costs · the scholarship's value and timeline · the player's level evidence · the family's goals.
**Reasoning:** Rules for moving minors internationally are strict, and an honest assessment starts there. A scholarship can be a development pathway, not a dead end.
**Decision:** **STAY → IMPROVE FIRST**, with a clear view of what would need to be true to revisit Europe later.
